## Audited the remaining reviewer surfaces for cap-style micromanagement

Follow-up sweep after v0.29.0. Two changes, three deliberate non-changes.

### Fixed: rate-limit advice traded review quality for cost

`claude-cli`, `codex-cli`, and `grok-cli` all told the lead to answer a rate limit with "a smaller prompt" / "a simpler prompt"; claude-cli also offered "switching to the project policy's lower-cost tier temporarily". Same instinct as the $5 cap — shrink the review to fit the constraint. A rate limit is service unavailability: wait for the window or swap reviewer. This also contradicted `pre-merge-review`, which already classified rate limit as blocker taxonomy #1.

### Fixed: CALIBRATION implied a finding count

All four adversarial prompts said "Prefer one strong finding over several weak ones." Anti-filler intent is right, but a reviewer with eight real findings could read it as pressure to trim. Now: "Do not pad the list with weak findings to look thorough, and do not trim real ones to look decisive. Report every material finding you can defend, however many that is." Applied byte-identically to codex-cli, claude-cli, grok-cli, and security-review — `tests/adversarial-prompt-sync.test.mjs` enforces that quartet identity, verified by md5 of the four blocks.

### Checked and deliberately left alone

- **`cross-model-convergence` round cap (default 4)** — a termination predicate for an unbounded loop, not a quality cap. Explicitly reasoned in the skill, with carve-outs so a verified defect at the cap still gets fixed and fix-induced regressions do not consume it.
- **`review-output.schema.json`** — no `maxItems` on `findings`; output is unbounded. Worth remembering: this is the obvious place a future cap would hide.
- **Model/effort defaults** — all three CLIs inherit the tool default and override only when the user or env sets one. No silent downgrade.
- **Review Targeting foreground/background heuristic** — routes output, never narrows scope.
