## Reframed the review timeout as a hang failsafe, not a budget

The user's argument: micromanaging reviewers with caps produces the real runaway — attempt 1 burns 10 min and 200k tokens and returns nothing, so attempt 2 runs with a bigger timeout, then attempt 3 with a split diff. Three dead attempts cost far more than the one uncapped review. Scope belongs in the prompt; the reviewer should be allowed to finish.

### Changes

- **Flat 30-min failsafe replaces tiered timeouts.** The old `600000` / `1200000` / `1800000` sizing ladder ("10 min simple, 20 min deep, 30 min large diff") is gone from `claude-cli`, `codex-cli`, `grok-cli`, and both reviewer prompts. Every review invocation now sets `1800000`, framed as "catch a process that hung or died — nothing else".
- **Killed the re-run escalation.** `reviewer-prompts/{claude,codex}.md` said "first raise the Bash tool's timeout and re-run", and the CLI skills said "split the review into smaller chunks rather than retrying". Both are gone. A tripped failsafe now means the process is broken → blocker taxonomy #1, block, let the human decide.
- **Timeout moved from "Not a blocker" to a blocker trigger.** "Not a blocker" now reads: "A long run. A review that takes 10-20+ minutes is working, not stuck. Wait for it."
- **Two new red flags in `pre-merge-review/SKILL.md`:** *Re-run a reviewer that already burned a full attempt* (the one permitted repeat is the single parse-failure retry) and *Cap the reviewer to control cost*.

### New guard test

`tests/reviewer-uncapped.test.mjs` (6 tests) — no recipe passes `--max-turns` or `--max-budget-usd` a value; every reviewer doc pins `1800000`, says "failsafe", and carries no `600000`/`1200000`; no doc contains re-run escalation wording. Scanner unit tests prove the regexes catch the exact wording removed and do not fire on the prohibition phrasing that replaced it.
